Research Statement

My research and expertise focuses on food safety, environmental sustainability, and fermentation to develop innovative bioproducts while reducing waste. I integrate microbial fermentation and omics-based strategies to transform food waste into high-value compounds like bacteriocins and nootropic therapeutics, contributing to a circular economy. Additionally, my work in environmental toxicology explored water quality, contaminant monitoring, and microbial ecology using next-generation sequencing and advanced analytical techniques. By bridging food science, biotechnology, and environmental stewardship, my goal is to position Canada as a leader in sustainable innovation and regulatory advancements.

Research Publications

1. Flavor or safety? A critical review of managing processing-induced toxicants in flavored vegetable oils

Food Control (2025)

This review examined the formation of harmful processing contaminants in flavoured vegetable oils and the trade-off between flavour development and food safety. It highlighted how high-temperature processing can generate compounds such as polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, 3-MCPD esters, and glycidyl esters. These findings emphasize the need to optimize roasting, refining, and flavouring conditions to produce high-quality oils while minimizing potential health risks.
2. Advancing ethanol quality amidst a pandemic: a case study from Saskatchewan

Canadian Journal of Chemistry (2025)

This study explores how Saskatchewan’s ethanol industry adapted during the COVID-19 pandemic to upgrade fuel-grade ethanol for hand sanitizer production. The Saskatchewan Structural Sciences Centre developed quality assurance methods using ozonation, chemical treatments, carbon filtration, and optimized distillation to meet Health Canada standards. This initiative fostered collaboration between academia, government, and industry, turning a crisis into an opportunity for scientific innovation and economic sustainability.

5. Transesterification catalyst strength as observed by solution-based oxygen-17 nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy

Authors: Purdy, S. K., Shen, J., Zhu, J., Tse, T. J., Reaney, M. J. T., Nie, Q., He, D., Hu, C., Zheng, J., Lei, F., Zhang, Q.

Canadian Journal of Chemistry (2024)

This study utilized natural abundance 17O-NMR spectroscopy to analyze homogeneous transesterification catalysts for fatty acid methyl ester production. Alkoxide catalysts, prepared using various hydroxides and alcohols, exhibited downfield shifts in 17O-NMR spectra, correlating with catalyst ion concentration. The strongest electron deshielding and fastest catalytic activity were observed in potassium methoxide, with less pronounced effects in less polar solvents like isopropanol.

7. Determination of vanillin compounds in oilseed using solid phase extraction with ultra-performance liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry

This study developed a solid-phase extraction and UPLC–MS/MS method to identify and quantify vanillin, methylvanillin, and ethylvanillin in oilseeds. 8. Water-modification of micro- and nano-adsorbents: A strategy for enhancing biodiesel refining efficiency

Authors: Zhou, L., Tse, T. J., Shen, J., Meda, V., Reaney, M. J. T.

Energy Conversion and Management: X (2024)

This study compared nanometer-scale (nm) and micrometer-scale (μm) silica (SiO2) and alumina (Al2O3) adsorbents for refining crude fatty acid methyl esters (FAME). While nm-adsorbents did not retain water, they led to higher FAME losses and required electrostatic field treatment for effective separation. Despite challenges in recovery, nm-adsorbents offer potential for improving biodiesel refining efficiency and sustainability by reducing the need for extensive wet washing.

10. Electrostatic field as an emergent technology in refining crude vegetable oils: a review

Authors: Li, Z., Tse, T. J.,, Chicilo, F., Meda, V., Reaney, M. J. T.

Critical Reviews in Food Science and Nutrition (2023)

This review examines traditional and novel refining technologies for vegetable oils and fatty acid esters (FAEs), highlighting the challenges of conventional methods, such as waste production and nutrient loss. Nano-adsorbents and electrostatic field (E-field) technologies show promise in efficiently removing contaminants while minimizing oil loss, though recovering spent nano-adsorbents remains a challenge. 11. Uptake of Flaxseed Dietary Linusorbs Modulates Regulatory Genes Including Induction of Heat Shock Proteins and Apoptosis

Authors: Shim, Y. Y., Tse, T. J.,, Saini, A. K., Kim, Y. J., Reaney, M. J. T.

Foods (2022)

This study explores the bioactivity of linusorbs (LOs), cyclic peptides found in flaxseed, which exhibit immunosuppressive and pro-apoptotic effects in human cancer cells and nematodes. LOs were absorbed after flaxseed consumption, induced heat shock proteins, and modulated regulatory genes, suggesting a role in apoptosis. Their ability to influence gene expression highlights their potential as biologically active compounds for cancer therapy.

This study investigated the fermentation of whole flaxseed with Lactobacillaceae cultures to remove cyanogenic glycosides (CGs), which pose health risks and limit flaxseed marketability. 15. Electrostatic field and nano-adsorbent refining of fatty acid methyl esters

Authors: Zhou, L., Tse, T. J., Chicilo, F., Shen, J., Meda, V., Reaney, M. J. T.

Journal of Cleaner Production (2022)

This study explored electrostatic field (E-field) and nano-adsorbents as novel methods for refining crude fatty acid methyl esters (FAME), reducing soap content while minimizing FAME loss. E-field treatment alone reduced soap content, while nano-adsorbents (Al2O3, SiO2, TiO2) further improved purification with minimal agitation and at ambient temperature. The process preserved FAME composition and allowed for adsorbent reuse, presenting a more sustainable alternative to conventional refining methods.

Toxicology Reports (2021)

This review examines the risks associated with alcohol-based hand rubs (ABHRs) formulated with technical-grade ethanol, which were temporarily permitted during the COVID-19 pandemic. Increased vigilance by regulatory agencies and consumers is necessary to ensure product safety and compliance.

This study compared the bioavailability of enriched secoisolariciresinol diglucoside (SDG) and SDG polymer in rats following oral administration. 28. Characterization of Trimethylolpropane-Based Biolubricant

Authors: Nie, J., Shim, Y. Y., Shen, J., Tse, T. J., Reaney, M. J. T.

European Journal of Lipid Science and Technology (2020)

This study compared the oxidative stability index (OSI) of fatty acid methyl esters (FAME) and trimethylolpropane esters (TMPE) from five vegetable oils, finding that processing reduced stability due to excess FAME and antioxidant loss. Low-temperature flow properties of TMPE from four oils were analyzed using proton NMR, showing increased molecular mobility with rising temperature. Extrapolated T2 relaxation data suggested that molecular motion largely ceased between 232 K and 239 K.
29. Synthesis of Trimethylolpropane Esters by Base-Catalyzed Transesterification

Authors: Nie, J., Shen, J., Shim, Y. Y., Tse, T. J., Reaney, M. J. T.

European Journal of Lipid Science and Technology (2019)

This study optimized a reverse addition reaction method for synthesizing trimethylolpropane (TMP) esters from fatty acid methyl esters (FAME) for use as biolubricants. Vegetable oils were transesterified to produce FAME, which was then reacted with TMP under vacuum using a potassium carbonate catalyst, with reaction progress monitored by 1H-NMR. The resulting TMP-based biolubricants were characterized for oxidative stability and low-temperature performance, demonstrating their potential as high-performance, sustainable lubricant base oils.

31. Spatial and temporal trends in poly- and per-fluorinated compounds in the Laurentian Great Lakes Erie, Ontario and St. Clair

Authors: Codling, G., Sturchio, N. C., Rockne, K. J., Li, A., Peng, H., Tse, T. J., Jones, P. D., Giesy, J. P.

Environmental Pollution (2018)

This study examined temporal and spatial trends of 22 poly- and perfluorinated (PFAS) compounds in sediments from Lakes Erie, Ontario, and St. Clair, revealing higher concentrations near urban areas. Perfluoro-n-butanoic acid (PFBA) and perfluoro-n-hexanoic acid (PFHxA) were frequently detected, suggesting shifts in PFAS usage. Sediment cores indicated regulatory impacts on PFAS deposition, but factors such as bioturbation and gas ebullition in Lake Erie complicated trend analysis.
32. Combining High-Throughput Sequencing of sedaDNA and Traditional Paleolimnological Techniques To Infer Historical Trends in Cyanobacterial Communities

Authors: Tse, T. J., Doig, L. E., Tang, S., Zhang, X., Sun, W., Wiseman, S. B., Feng, C. X., Liu, H., Giesy, J. P., Hecker, M., Jones, P. D.

Environmental Science & Technology (2018)

This study utilized sedimentary ancient DNA (sedaDNA) and high-throughput sequencing (16S rRNA) alongside traditional paleolimnological analyses to reconstruct long-term cyanobacterial community trends in a northern Canadian Great Plains reservoir. Findings revealed significant community shifts over decades, with recent increases in potentially toxic cyanobacteria like Dolichospermum, correlating with microcystin synthetase genes and paleoenvironmental proxies. This approach demonstrates the potential of sedaDNA for tracking historical water quality changes and informing lake and reservoir management strategies.
33. Long-term spatial trends in sedimentary algal pigments in a narrow river-valley reservoir, Lake Diefenbaker, Canada

Authors: Tse, T. J., Doig, L. E., Leavitt, P. R., Quiñones-Rivera, Z. J., Codling, G., Lucas, B. T., Liver, K., Giesy, J. P., Wheater, H., Jones, P. D.

Journal of Great Lakes Research (2015)

This study examined spatial and temporal trends in algal biomass and community composition in Lake Diefenbaker, a long, narrow river-valley reservoir in Saskatchewan, Canada. Sediment core analyses revealed increasing algal biomass down-reservoir, with peak production in mid-reservoir, and a significant biomass rise after 1990, especially post-2000. Findings challenge conventional reservoir trophic development models, suggesting early light limitation and highlighting the need for paleolimnological studies across hydrologic zones in large reservoirs.
34. Reconstructing long-term trends in municipal sewage discharge into a small lake in northern Manitoba, Canada

Authors: Tse, T. J., Codling, G., Jones, P. D., Thoms, K., Liver, K., Giesy, J. P., Wheater, H., Doig, L. E.

Chemosphere (2013)

This study analyzed sediment cores from Ross Lake, Flin Flon, Manitoba, to reconstruct historical contamination from industrial effluent and municipal sewage discharge. Peak concentrations of fecal sterols, including coprostanol, were detected in sediments corresponding to the 1930s, supporting evidence of historical sewage input before treatment improvements in 1951. Trends in terrestrial phytosterols suggest increased organic matter flux, likely linked to regional deforestation from logging and fire.
